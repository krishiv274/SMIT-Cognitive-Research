## Study title

Investigating the Effects of Numerical and Visual Interference on Spatial Working Memory and Cognitive Performance Among University Students (SMIT — Spatial Memory Interference Task)

## Purpose of the study

We are studying how people remember the positions of shapes for a few seconds while doing other brief tasks. The results will be used for a course research report.

## What you will be asked to do

- Provide your age, gender, academic course and email address.
- Read instructions and complete 6 practice trials.
- Complete 60 short trials (about 6–7 seconds each) in three blocks with short breaks. In each trial you will see a grid with some filled squares, sometimes do a quick true/false task, and then decide whether a second grid is the same as the first.
- Total time: approximately **15–20 minutes**.

## Voluntary participation and right to withdraw

Participation is entirely voluntary. You may stop at any time without giving a reason and without any penalty (press **Esc** and tell the researcher, or simply close the window). You may also ask for your data to be withdrawn and deleted after taking part, up to **[DATE — e.g. the date of report submission]**, by contacting the researcher and quoting your participant ID.

## Data collected

- **Demographic data:** age, gender (including "prefer not to answer"), academic course.
- **Contact data:** email address (required by the course assignment).
- **Task data:** your responses, accuracy and reaction times for each trial; technical information about the browser and screen (e.g. refresh rate, screen size) needed to check timing quality.

## Confidentiality and data handling

- You will be identified in the research data only by an automatically generated **participant ID**.
- Your email address is stored **separately** from your task data and is accessible only to the named student researchers. It will not appear in any report, figure or presentation.
- Data are stored locally on the researchers' computer **[or: on a password-protected university server]** and are not sent to third-party services.
- Only aggregated, de-identified results will be reported.
- Contact details will be deleted **[e.g. at the end of the semester]**; de-identified data may be retained for **[e.g. one year]** for assessment purposes, then deleted.

## Risks and benefits

This is a minimal-risk study. Some people may find the tasks mildly tiring or frustrating, or experience brief eye strain from looking at a screen. The task contains rapidly appearing (but not flashing/flickering) grids; if you have photosensitive epilepsy or any condition that makes screen tasks uncomfortable, please do not take part. There is no direct benefit to you other than learning about cognitive psychology research.
